The problem

Modern libraries are accidental.

Years of Epic free Thursdays, Prime Gaming, GOG giveaways, Fanatical bundles, and itch mega-bundles — and no storefront helps you audit what you actually own.

Pain

The five-launcher tax. Steam, Epic, GOG, PSN, Xbox — every launcher is a silo. “What should I play tonight?” means checking five places.

Pain

The duplicate buy. Deal alerts have no ownership context — half the “great deals” are for games you already own elsewhere.

Pain

The itch.io blackout. 1,400-item bundles where you cannot tell games from assets, zines, or soundtracks.

Pain

Decision paralysis dressed as abundance. 200+ titles you never bought intentionally — and zero tools built for that reality.

Pain

The forgotten account. A dormant Epic login, an old GOG profile, a PSN from a console you sold — every reconnect surfaces games you forgot you owned years ago.

Counter-positioning: storefronts profit when you cannot see across stores. BAKLOG runs on your machine and refuses to be one.

Slash line

AVG · OBP · SLG · OPS

Your finish rate as a batting average. Slugging weights epics vs quick clears. OPS is the headline number for backlog productivity — in classic .333 notation.

WAR & BV+

Every game has a value

Wins Above Replacement scores each title against the Mendoza line (median backlog rating). BV+ normalizes rating per hour invested. The library table shows a WAR pill on standouts.

The accidental-library economy is structural: Epic weekly frees, Prime monthly drops, GOG giveaways, Fanatical keys, Wario64 alerts — libraries balloon while playtime stays flat.

  • PC gaming market $40B+ — niche workflow tools can sustain a business
  • High daily Steam usage supports recurring engagement (deal radar, picks)
  • Audit-to-claim loop: surfacing the backlog pulls users back to active giveaways — claims grow libraries and feed deal/affiliate intent
  • Free-claimer persona has near-zero willingness to pay upfront — ads + optional upgrade align

Business model

Free forever. Ads when you're hunting deals.

Lead model: Freemium · free + ads — sponsored deal slots + house/affiliate first; $2–$4/mo No-ads + cloud sync upgrade for users who want them gone. Metered achievement/trophy enrichment — deep API pulls billed by usage (credit packs or bundled in paid tier).

Lead candidate

Freemium · free + ads

Ad-supported dashboard (deal cards, footer). Context-only ads — no behavioral tracking. Upgrade tier kills ad code paths, not just hides them.

ARR floor math: 50k MAU × ($0.50 RPM + 1.5% × $3 upgrade) ≈ $25k–$50k + affiliate uplift.

End-state: Hybrid

Free + ads local · $2–$4/mo No-ads + cloud · affiliate on every deal card. Three diversified lines. Ship freemium half first; layer cloud when retention proves out.

Usage-based

Metered achievement / trophy enrichment

Steam achievements, PSN/Xbox trophies, RetroAchievements — deep per-title sync (rarity, unlock times, tier breakdown) costs real upstream API calls. Free tier: cached summary + slow lane. Paid: priority refresh, higher limits, or $3 credit packs for one-off deep pulls.

Pay for throughput, not platform data. Requests stay on the user's machine with their credentials.

Trust

There is no BAKLOG server to breach.

This is the product moat, not a footnote: credentials stay on your machine (OS keyring + AES-256-GCM secrets). BAKLOG fetches through your browser session and your IP, opening a direct channel from each store to your PC — no server hop in between. Bug bundles are whitelist-only — no library JSON, no credentials. Competitors need your data on their servers; BAKLOG does not.

Risk & ethics

Credible by design.

  • ToS framing: Automating requests you could make in your own browser, on your machine — see SECURITY.md
  • Ad ethics: Disclosure on every slot; house/affiliate before third-party networks; no behavioral tracking
  • Data honesty: Drift guard, atomic writes, fetcher chips ≠ fake green connections
  • Telemetry: Hybrid local-only — opt-in; bug bundle whitelist excludes personal data
